# Research method

## Programme order

1. Freeze the ontological commitments and operational requirements.
2. Translate prior failures into candidate-selection gates.
3. Construct multiple stable saturated-medium candidates.
4. Test boundedness, background stability, causality, and excitation spectra.
5. Derive screens and their composition law for surviving candidates.
6. Test particle quantum mechanics and effective geometry separately.
7. Permit observational phenomenology only after a response law is frozen.

Later stages may not be used to tune an upstream candidate around a failed
gate. A stopped dependency remains visible in the result and claim ledgers.

## Preregister before selection

For every derivation, numerical audit, or observation test, freeze before the
decisive calculation:

- the candidate set;
- input provenance and viewed-data boundary;
- equations, units, and parameter mapping;
- exclusions and quality criteria;
- primary and sensitivity analyses;
- success, revision, rejection, and stopping rules; and
- the artifact list required for completion.

Retrospective work must be labelled retrospective. Internal cross-validation
of a viewed sample is a diagnostic, not confirmation.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. exact derivation with explicit assumptions;
2. deterministic symbolic or numerical verification;
3. controlled simulation with convergence and sensitivity checks;
4. excluded-system prediction from independently frozen inputs;
5. retrospective fit or analogy.

Lower levels may motivate a candidate but cannot silently inherit the status of
higher levels.

## Required artifact pattern

Each completed issue should normally provide:

- preregistration or retrospective protocol;
- input/provenance manifest;
- derivation and dimensional ledger;
- instrument and focused tests;
- machine-readable receipt;
- result table or negative ledger;
- decision and status report; and
- completion audit verifying the declared scope.

## Claim language

- **Derived:** follows from stated inputs without fitted missing structure.
- **Conditional:** follows after an explicit additional postulate.
- **Compatible:** can reproduce the observation but is not uniquely supported.
- **Supported:** passes a frozen support rule against an outcome sample that was
  not used to choose the rule.
- **Unsupported:** a specified tested candidate fails its frozen support rule.
- **Underdetermined/data-limited:** the mechanism could not be identified or the
  required test could not be executed.
- **Falsified/rejected:** a valid test contradicts a necessary prediction.

Do not use `unsupported` as a synonym for `untested`.
